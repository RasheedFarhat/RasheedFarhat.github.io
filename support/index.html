How I work a queue

Diagnose it, explain it, write it down.

Own it through resolution

A first response is not a fix. I confirm the problem is actually gone with the person who reported it before I close anything, because a quiet ticket and a solved ticket are not the same thing.

Escalate with the diagnosis attached

When something has to go to another person, it goes with what I already tried, what I ruled out, and how to reproduce it. Passing along the original symptom just makes someone else start over.

Translate in both directions

Students, faculty, and staff arrive with very different technical vocabularies. Most of the work is turning "it stopped working" into something specific enough to act on, then explaining the cause back in language that person can use.

Fix the pattern, not just the ticket

I tracked which questions kept recurring and maintained the departmental reference documentation the rest of the office worked from. Forty people asking the same thing is a documentation problem wearing a support costume.

The obvious question

Why does someone with CySA+ want a support seat?

The straight answer.

Because the support desk is where I learned to do the part of security work that actually matters, which is talking to a human being who is stuck and figuring out what is really going on. I like being the person who gets someone working again. I would rather be excellent at that first than be a mediocre analyst early. The certifications are not a plan to leave in six months. They are why I notice the ticket that is not really a printer problem.

What I have not done.

My support environment was Windows 10 and 11 desktop. I have not administered Active Directory, Exchange, a Microsoft 365 tenant, Intune or another MDM platform, or a named ITSM product such as ServiceNow, Jira, or Zendesk. I have not done imaging or deployment at fleet scale, and I have not worked in production cloud. I would rather you know that from the page than find it in an interview.
